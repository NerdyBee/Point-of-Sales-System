1. Executive Summary
This document defines the requirements and recommended technology architecture for a modern Point of 
Sale (POS) and business management platform. The solution is intended for restaurants, bars, cafés, hotels, 
lounges, bakeries, supermarkets, pharmacies and other retail businesses, with strong support for Nigerian 
operating conditions.
The platform will combine fast touchscreen sales, restaurant table management, kitchen and bar order 
routing, inventory, purchasing, customers, suppliers, payments, cash shifts, analytics and multi-branch 
administration. It will be delivered as a secure multi-tenant product so that each subscribing business 
operates in an isolated workspace.
PRIMARY RECOMMENDATION
Use TypeScript across the frontend, desktop terminal, backend and optional mobile application. Combine 
React, Electron, Node.js, Express, PostgreSQL, Prisma, SQLite and Socket.IO. Start with a modular monolith 
and introduce Redis only when scale or background processing requires it.
2. Product Objectives
 Provide a fast and intuitive sales experience optimized for touchscreens, tablets, laptops and desktop POS 
terminals.
 Support restaurant, hospitality and retail workflows from a shared configurable platform.
 Continue essential sales operations during unstable or unavailable internet connectivity.
 Give owners real-time visibility into sales, stock, employees, branches, cash and profitability.
 Protect financial records through permissions, approvals, audit trails and reliable transaction controls.
 Allow multiple independent businesses to subscribe without exposing one tenant’s information to 
another.
 Localize currency, payment integrations, receipt sharing and operational patterns for Nigeria.

5. Multi-Tenant and Multi-Branch Architecture
Each registered business will operate inside a logically isolated tenant workspace. Every business record must 
carry a tenant identifier, and tenant-aware authorization must be enforced in the API and database access 
layer.
 Separate products, menus, customers, suppliers, employees, branches, terminals, transactions and 
reports.
 Tenant-specific logo, colours, receipt information, taxes, service charges, currency and business rules.
 Branch-specific stock, pricing, users, registers, printers, kitchen stations and reporting.
 Subscription limits for branches, users, terminals, features, integrations and storage.
 No cross-tenant access, including through reports, exports, URLs, background jobs or synchronization.
6. POS Sales Terminal
The POS terminal should prioritize speed, accuracy and minimal interaction. The main screen should place 
product categories and search beside a persistent current-order panel.
 Product categories, image cards, search, SKU lookup and barcode scanning.
 Add items, change quantity, attach notes, select variants, modifiers and add-ons.
 Calculate configurable tax, service charges, line discounts and order discounts.
 Assign a customer, customer group, price list, loyalty profile or account balance.
 Hold and retrieve orders; save quotations; duplicate approved orders.

17. Offline Operation and Synchronization
Offline capability must be designed into the transaction model from the beginning, even if full 
synchronization is implemented after the stable online workflow. A desktop terminal should maintain a 
secure local SQLite database and a durable synchronization queue.
 Create orders, manage tables, record permitted payments and print receipts while offline.
 Route tickets to locally accessible kitchen or bar printers and displays.
 Assign globally unique transaction identifiers at the terminal.
 Upload queued records automatically when connectivity returns.
 Use idempotency controls so the same transaction cannot be posted twice.
 Display connection, last synchronization and pending-record status.
 Record synchronization failures and conflicts for controlled retry or manager review.
 Prevent server-side edits from silently overwriting newer terminal activity.
18. Security and Audit Requirements
 Secure password login and quick PIN login for authorized terminal users.
 JWT access and refresh tokens, secure session controls and device management.
 Role-, branch- and action-based permissions with default-deny behavior.
 Optional two-factor authentication for owners and administrators.
 Encryption for sensitive data in transit and appropriate protection at rest.
 Rate limiting, failed-login protection, input validation and secure headers.

25. Why Electron Is Recommended
A browser-only POS can face restrictions when communicating with thermal printers, cash drawers, customer 
displays and other local devices. Electron allows the team to reuse React and TypeScript while adding a 
controlled desktop layer for local device integration, SQLite storage, automatic updates and background 
synchronization.
The administration portal should remain a normal web application. Kitchen displays can be browser-based 
progressive web applications where local printer requirements do not demand a desktop wrapper.

29. Architecture Strategy
Begin with a modular monolith: one deployable API organized into clearly separated business domains such 
as tenants, branches, users, catalog, orders, payments, inventory, purchasing, registers, reports and 
subscriptions. This keeps development and deployment manageable while preserving clear module 
boundaries.
 Avoid microservices in the first release.
 Share TypeScript types and Zod schemas where doing so does not expose server-only information.
 Use database transactions for payment, stock and cash operations that must succeed or fail together.
 Create a versioned API and migration strategy before pilot deployment.
 Introduce queues, caching and separate services only after measured operational need.

 Backup restoration, audit integrity and deployment rollback testing.
 User acceptance testing with owners, managers, cashiers, waiters and kitchen staff.
